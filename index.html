DEBRIEFING STATEMENT

[IRB-approved study title]

Thank you for participating in this study.

The purpose of this research is to examine how people learn which pictures go together, and what they do when they meet a picture they have never learned. When people see an unfamiliar item next to familiar ones, they often reason “it can’t be that one, I already know what that goes with,” and choose the unfamiliar option instead. This is called choice by exclusion. We are also interested in whether a single such choice is enough for people to learn the new pairing, which is called fast mapping.

During the task, the number of picture pairs you learned grew over the session. At several points, a new picture appeared at the top in rounds where you did not receive feedback. These rounds measured how people choose when they have no learned answer. We are comparing when these choices appear as the number of learned pairs grows, and comparing human performance with that of other species, such as pigeons, that we have tested on a very similar task.

The instructions said that in some rounds you would “find out later” whether you were right. Those rounds had no single correct answer that you could have learned, so feedback was not given; we withheld it so that it would not change how you responded in later rounds. We did not otherwise provide false information about the study.

Please do not discuss the specific purpose of this study with other students who may participate in the future, as knowing this information in advance could influence their behavior and affect the results of the study.
